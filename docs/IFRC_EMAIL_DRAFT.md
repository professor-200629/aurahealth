**Subject:** Use of the IFRC 2020 first aid guidelines in a free, non-commercial prototype (AuraHealth)

Dear Global First Aid Reference Centre team,

My name is [your name]. I am an independent developer in India building **AuraHealth**, a free, open-source
research prototype that helps a bystander give first aid while waiting for an ambulance, and then hands
emergency staff a timed record of what was done.

The first-aid steps in the app are short plain-English adaptations of the **International first aid,
resuscitation and education guidelines 2020**: CPR, recovery position, choking, severe bleeding, chest pain,
stroke recognition, snakebite and seizure. Each guide credits the IFRC and lists the pages it comes from. The
steps are fixed text; no AI writes or changes them. Medicine doses are left out. The app is labelled as a
prototype, and the steps are being reviewed by a doctor.

The prototype is free, has no ads or sales, and is entered in the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon (which has
prize money). Could you confirm whether this counts as non-commercial use under the guidelines' terms, and let
me know if you would like anything changed in how the IFRC is credited?

Thank you for making these guidelines available.
